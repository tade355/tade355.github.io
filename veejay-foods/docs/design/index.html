01 · Concept

Counter queues are the problem. Pre-ordering is the fix.

2 / 19

The problem

Veejay Foods serves homemade African meals, pastries and local cuisine. Like any popular outlet in a busy city it loses sales and goodwill at peak hours: long counter queues, orders shouted over a crowd, cash handling, and “where is my order?” with no answer. Customers abandon the queue; staff get overwhelmed in bursts and idle in between.

The idea

Let customers choose a pickup point and a time slot, pay in advance, and walk in to scan a QR code. The kitchen sees a live, time-ordered queue, so demand is spread across slots instead of arriving in a rush. Money is settled before food is made, and every order has an auditable trail.

One-line promise

“Pick it, pay it, collect it — no queue.”

Launch area: Abuja Municipal (AMAC)

Pickup points are limited to Abuja Municipal Area Council districts — for example Wuse, Garki, Maitama, Asokoro, Jabi, Utako and the Central Area. Other areas and cities are out of scope for now; the data model already supports adding more pickup points later without code changes.

Why it works for each side

Customer

  • No waiting in line
  • Choose the exact pickup time
  • Pay by card, transfer or USSD
  • Live status + push notifications
  • Wrong order? Report with a photo, get refunded

Kitchen / counter

  • Orders arrive already paid
  • Slot capacity caps the rush
  • Clear New → Preparing → Ready board
  • Scan-to-collect, no disputes
  • Works for lunch and evening peaks

Owner

  • Sales by day, outlet and item
  • Sold-out toggles per outlet
  • Refund control, audit trail
  • Menu, prices and hours without a developer

Proposed success metrics

  • Median counter wait at pickup < 2 min
  • Cart → paid conversion
  • Refund rate < 2% of orders
  • Repeat orders within 14 days
Targets to agree with the business — none measured yet.

If something goes wrong

  • Paid but screen didn’t update — the app asks the server to re-check with Paystack; the webhook is the safety net.
  • Phone dies at the counter — staff type the 6-character order code instead of scanning.
  • Wrong or cold food — report in-app with a photo; admin approves and the refund goes back to the original payment method.

Design rules that follow from the users

  • A user with a paid, uncollected order is never signed out — they always have their QR.
  • Every critical button is ≥ 50 px tall; the counter UI works one-handed.
  • Prices are integers in kobo end-to-end — no rounding surprises in ₦.
  • Network failures never look like “logged out” or “payment failed”.

05 · Snapshots · Pay

Pick a slot, pay securely, done

7 / 19

What happens when “Pay” is tapped

1
Order is created on the server

Prices are re-read from the database (the client can’t set them). The chosen slot is locked and re-checked for capacity. The order sits in PENDING_PAYMENT for 15 minutes.

2
Paystack hosted checkout opens

In a secure in-app browser, with card, bank transfer and USSD enabled. Card numbers are entered on Paystack’s page — never in our app or API.

3
Paystack calls our webhook

We verify the signature over the raw body, then check reference, amount and currency before moving the order to PLACED.

4
The app confirms with the server

On return it asks /orders/:id/verify, which asks Paystack directly. Webhook and verify share one code path, so they can never disagree.

Edge cases handled

  • Webhook arrives twice (or six times at once) → applied once, one push notification.
  • Amount or currency differs → order stays unpaid; payment flagged.
  • Payment lands after the hold expired → automatically refunded, order stays cancelled.
  • Server error mid-webhook → returns 500 so Paystack retries; nothing half-applied.
  • Closed browser / lost signal → order screen keeps checking and offers “Pay now” until the hold lapses.

05 · Snapshots · Collect

Always know where your order is — and prove it’s yours

8 / 19

Notifications

A push is sent on every status change:

  • ✅ Order placed — payment received
  • 🍳 Preparing — kitchen started
  • 🛍️ Ready for pickup — show your QR
  • 😋 Collected — thank you

Tapping a notification opens that order. Delivery via Expo Push (FCM on Android, APNs on iOS). Needs a development / production build, not Expo Go.

QR design

Payload nbg1.<orderId>.<HMAC> — derived, not stored, so it can always be re-rendered and can’t be guessed.

05 · Snapshots · After-sales

When something’s wrong, fixing it is easy

9 / 19

The refund journey

  1. Customer reports a paid order — one open report per order; photo uploads are limited to JPG / PNG / WebP, 5 MB.
  2. Admin sees it in Complaints & refunds with the photo and what was ordered (see next pages).
  3. Approve (full or partial) → refund is sent to Paystack against the original transaction, so money returns to the original card / account. Amount is capped at what was paid.
  4. Paystack confirms asynchronously → our webhook marks the refund SUCCESS and the payment REFUNDED.
  5. If Paystack refuses → the complaint reopens automatically so an admin can retry. Double-clicks can’t double-refund.

Account safety

Signing out is a deliberate choice only. Sessions end involuntarily only after 30 days of inactivity or a detected token-theft pattern — and never while an order is active.

07 · Architecture

Small, boring, and safe with money

13 / 19

Why this stack

One language (TypeScript) across API, app and dashboard; Expo gives iOS + Android from one codebase and managed push; PostgreSQL gives transactions and row locks that money and slot-capacity need.

Seams for change

Paystack, SMS, push and storage are interfaces injected into services — swapped for fakes in tests and replaceable (e.g. Flutterwave, another SMS gateway, S3) without touching business logic.

Scale

A single small Node instance and a managed Postgres comfortably serve a multi-outlet restaurant brand. The 60-second expiry job and 5-second polling are the only periodic load.

One source of truth for “is it paid?”

14 / 19

Rules enforced in code

  • No skipping or going backwards; terminal states are final.
  • A paid order can never be cancelled — only refunded.
  • Each transition is a guarded UPDATE … WHERE status = from, so two simultaneous taps apply once.
  • Staff can set Preparing / Ready; Collected only via the QR scan (or the order-code fallback).

09 · Data model

Sixteen tables, money in integer kobo

15 / 19

Integrity by design

Order lines snapshot name and price; payments are append-only with a unique Paystack reference; every status change writes an OrderEvent.

Idempotency

WebhookEvent.eventKey is unique, so a re-delivered webhook is detected at the database, even across concurrent requests.

Time zone

All instants are UTC; opening hours and slots are computed in Africa/Lagos (UTC+1, no DST) in one module.

13 · Risks & decisions

What to decide next

19 / 19

Top risks

SMS deliverability
OTP is the front door. Sender IDs need registration; carriers filter.
Store review delays
Payments + login can trigger extra questions. Keep a 1-week buffer.
Staff adoption at peak
Needs a counter device, training, and the code-entry fallback.
Slot tuning
Wrong capacity either starves sales or recreates the queue.
Service-area expectations
Abuja Municipal only at launch. Say so clearly in the app, the store listing and marketing.
Image storage
Photos are on local disk today; lost on redeploy until moved to object storage.

Decisions needed

  1. Confirm logo files and brand colours (sampled from the logo you shared).
